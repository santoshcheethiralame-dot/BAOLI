THE SURFACE

    Toorji Ka Jhalra is real. The shrine in the water is not — we placed it.

    What is real and what is not

    We changed things.

    The well is a scan of Toorji Ka Jhalra in Jodhpur, built in the 1740s by the queen consort of Maharaja Abhay Singh. The architecture you descended is really there — the flights on three sides, the pavilion wall, the jharokhas and the chhatris.

    Almost nothing else is.

    • The shrineOurs. A separate temple model, placed in the water. There is no shrine at Toorji Ka Jhalra.
    • The lingamOurs. A third model, set on the sanctum floor.
    • The water levelChosen. Held at roughly two-thirds depth because it leaves the flights readable.
    • The stonePainted. The scan arrives untextured, so every colour is a shader — bleached at the rim, grimier with depth, algal at the waterline.
    • The desert and the duskOurs. The scan is a sectional model that ends in a slab.

    Why stepwells stopped

    A tap is faster than four hundred steps.

    Across the twentieth century, piped supply and borewells made the walk unnecessary. Many stepwells silted up, were fenced off, or were filled in. The ones that survived largely did so as monuments rather than as infrastructure.

    What was lost with them is harder to price: a cool public room in a hot city, open to anyone, that cost nothing to enter and did not belong to a household.
