# BAOLI — Direction

Shot list, typography system, and content architecture.

## HONESTY CONTRACT — read before writing any copy

This piece is **an interpretation, not a document.**

- The well is a scan of **Toorji Ka Jhalra, Jodhpur**. The architecture is real.
- **The shrine in the water is ours.** There is no such shrine at Toorji Ka
  Jhalra. It is a separate temple model we placed there.
- The water level, the palette, the dusk, the desert around it and the whole
  descent are art direction, not survey.

Two rules follow, and they are not negotiable:

1. **Never assert an invented specific as history.** The first draft of this
   document had the well becoming "a rubbish dump" and being "emptied by hand,
   in six months." Evocative, and made up. Cut. Only claims that are actually
   true of Toorji Ka Jhalra, or true of stepwells generally, may be stated
   flatly. Everything else is framed as ours.
2. **Declare the liberty inside the piece**, not just in the colophon — see
   Beat 12, where the admission becomes the ending.

This is a strength, not a compromise. A piece that says "we invented this and
here is why" is more interesting than one pretending to be a documentary.

The film runs **~7,200vh** in twelve beats (currently 850vh in one unbroken move).
The point is not more scroll. The point is **rhythm**: cinema alternates hold →
move → hold, and right now we only have move.

---

## THE GOVERNING RULES

1. **Text appears only during HOLDS.** While the camera moves, nothing on screen
   but the chrome (altimeter, act title). This single discipline is what makes a
   scroll site feel directed rather than like a brochure being pulled past.
2. **The lens changes.** We shoot everything at 52°. A real DP would not. Wide
   (65°) to establish, normal (45°) to travel, long (28°) to compress the
   flights into pure pattern. Lens change *is* a storytelling tool and it costs
   one line of code.
3. **One cut in the whole film.** Everything else is continuous. A single hard
   cut, placed at the plunge, will be the most violent thing on the page
   precisely because nothing else cuts.
4. **Withhold the money shot.** Every competing entry opens on its hero. We open
   on empty sand for 400vh. The reveal only lands if something was withheld.
5. **Scale contrast.** Wide → detail → wide. Never two consecutive beats at the
   same apparent scale.

## THE SHOT LIST

### BEAT 1 — COLD OPEN · "the ground" · 0 → 400vh · **HOLD**
- **Camera** locked off, 1.5 units above the desert, horizon dead centre. Lens **35°**.
- The well is *not in frame*. Just sand, heat shimmer, and the dust layer.
- Title assembles letter by letter over ~200vh of scroll. Nothing else moves.
- **Copy:** बावड़ी / **BAOLI** / *A machine built to pull the sky underground.*

### BEAT 2 — THE REVEAL · 400 → 900vh · **MOVE** (crane up + push)
- Camera cranes up and forward. The ground opens. Lens widens **35° → 65°**.
- The well resolves from a black rectangle into architecture.
- Title dissolves upward as the void enters frame.
- **Copy (single line, arrives at 780vh):** *In 1740 they cut a hole in the desert.*

### BEAT 3 — THE LIP · 900 → 1500vh · **HOLD** (imperceptible push)
- Camera at the rim, looking down the throat. Lens **45°**.
- First real text block, right column.
- **Copy:** *Toorji Ka Jhalra, Jodhpur. Built in the 1740s by the queen consort
  of Maharaja Abhay Singh — one of the last great stepwells cut in Rajasthan.*
- **Data:** `c.1740 · JODHPUR · RAJASTHAN`
- No depth or step-count figure here. Verify against a source before printing a
  number, or print none.

### BEAT 4 — THE PLUNGE · 1500 → 1900vh · **THE CUT**
- **Hard cut.** Camera is suddenly at −4m looking *up* at the rim, sky reduced to
  a bright rectangle. Lens **65°**.
- No text. No chapter title. Silence for 400vh.
- The only cut in the film. It should read as falling.

### BEAT 5 — THE FLIGHTS · 1900 → 2900vh · **MOVE** (lateral track)
- Camera tracks sideways across the stepped wall. Lens **28° — long**.
- Telephoto compression flattens the criss-cross into graphic pattern. This is
  the beat where the architecture stops being a place and becomes a *drawing*.
- **Copy (arrives late, 2600vh):** *Flights on every side, cut so the water could
  be reached at any level, in any season.*
- The "three thousand steps" figure from the first draft belongs to **Chand
  Baori**, a different well entirely. Do not attach it to this one.

### BEAT 6 — THE MATH · 2900 → 3500vh · **HOLD**
- Camera still, wide on a corner where two flights cross. Lens **50°**.
- **Annotated diagram**: SVG lines drawn over the canvas, pointing at real
  geometry — riser height, the 45° pitch, the monsoon fill line.
- **Copy:** the engineering. Monsoon capture, reduced evaporation, the ~8°C
  temperature drop at the bottom.

### BEAT 7 — THE GALLERIES · 3500 → 4300vh · **MOVE** (slow orbit, 40°)
- Camera orbits the shaft, passing jharokhas and chhatris in the near field.
- Foreground parallax: a chhatri passes close to lens.
- **Copy:** the human story — who came here, and that it was women's labour and
  a social space, not just infrastructure.

### BEAT 8 — THE DESCENT · 4300 → 5000vh · **MOVE** (drop, 2° roll)
- Camera falls. Walls close in, light narrows, dust thickens.
- Subtle roll — the only roll in the film.
- Altimeter accelerates; depth readout becomes the loudest element on screen.
- **Copy:** single short lines appearing and dissolving, one per 200vh.

### BEAT 9 — THE WATER · 5000 → 5700vh · **DECELERATE** + rack focus
- Camera slows hard and comes level with the surface. Lens **45°**.
- **Rack focus**: foreground water → shrine. Depth-of-field pass.
- **Copy:** *And it holds the sky.*

### BEAT 10 — THE SHRINE · 5700 → 6400vh · **MOVE** (30° orbit)
- Slow orbit around the shrine, reflection tracking beneath it.
- **Copy:** what a baoli *was* — not a well but a room, cool and dim, where
  water was treated as something owed reverence. True of stepwells generally,
  so it can be stated flatly.

### BEAT 11 — THE ADMISSION · 6400 → 6900vh · **HOLD** + grade drain
- Camera locked on the shrine. Colour drains toward grey over the full beat,
  dust stops, the water goes still. Everything the piece has been doing to
  seduce you switches off.
- **Copy:** *There is no shrine at the bottom of Toorji Ka Jhalra.*
  *We put one there.*

### BEAT 12 — THE REASON · 6900 → 7200vh · **HOLD** + grade restore
- Colour floods back, warmer than before. Camera rises fractionally.
- **Copy:** *A baoli is a place where water was treated as sacred.*
  *We only made it literal.*
- Hands off to the content sections below.

> This replaces a first draft in which the well was abandoned and restored on
> invented dates. The turn is now true: the piece admits its own fiction and
> then justifies it. Same structural reversal, none of the lying.

## TYPOGRAPHY & DIALOGUE SYSTEM

Four roles, already in the type stack:

| Role | Face | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Eyebrow** | Space Mono | 0.6rem, 0.3em tracking, uppercase — the act name |
| **Statement** | Bodoni Moda | clamp(2rem, 4.5vw, 4rem), the single line |
| **Body** | Archivo 300 | 0.95rem, max **42ch**, 1.7 line-height |
| **Data** | Space Mono | tabular-nums, the numbers and the altimeter |

**Motion:** in = per-line `clip-path` mask reveal, 700ms
`cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)`, 80ms stagger. Out = 300ms fade + 12px rise.
Never fade text in as a block — reveal it by line, like a title card.

**Placement rhythm:** alternate the column each beat — right, left, right,
centre. Static placement across twelve beats is what makes long scroll sites
feel like a slide deck.

**Hold discipline:** copy is bound to the beat's hold window and unmounts when
the camera starts moving again. It never rides along with a moving camera.

## AFTER THE FILM — the content sections

Below the well, real HTML. This is what turns it from a demo into a site, and
it is also what the hackathon brief explicitly asks for.

1. **THE SECTION** — an interactive SVG cross-section of the well. Drag to move
   the water line through the seasons and watch which flights go under. The
   single most "Awwwards" idea here: it teaches something no photograph can.
2. **THE FAMILY** — horizontal-scroll gallery: Chand Baori, Rani ki Vav, Adalaj,
   Toorji. Each with depth, date, storeys. Pinned horizontal run.
3. **WHY THEY DIED** — piped water and borewells made them redundant across the
   twentieth century; many silted up or were filled. General and true; no dates
   pinned to this well specifically.
4. **WHAT WE CHANGED** — replaces the invented restoration section. A plain,
   illustrated list of every liberty: the shrine is a separate model we placed,
   the water level is chosen, the palette is painted on an untextured scan, the
   desert and the dusk are ours. Owning this openly is more interesting than a
   fake restoration story, and it is the section a design jury will respect.
5. **COLOPHON** — tech list, model credits and licences, source link. Doubles as
   the hackathon's required technologies list. Leads with the honesty contract.

## INTERACTION BEATS

- **Sound toggle**, prominent, default off. Depth-reactive: lowpass sweeps
  8000 → 400Hz, reverb decay 0.8s → 7s as you descend.
- **Click the water** → ripple (built).
- **Release the camera** at the very end: after beat 12, scroll hands control to
  a slow free orbit around the shrine. The site stops driving and lets you look.
- **Reduced motion**: all twelve beats become static frames with the same copy.

---

## WHY THIS WINS RATHER THAN JUST BEING LONGER

- The cold open is a **risk** — 400vh of almost nothing. It is also the only way
  the reveal has any force.
- The single cut is the most memorable 400vh on the page *because* the other
  6,800 are continuous.
- The telephoto flights beat turns architecture into graphic pattern — the one
  frame people screenshot.
- The section diagram is the only part of the whole piece that **teaches**, and
  teaching is what separates a memorable site from a pretty one.
- The grade drain in beat 11 gives the piece a *turn*. Nothing else in this
  hackathon will have a narrative reversal.
